T21 alias method (published; synthetic fixture, not a circuit): Fs 48 kHz; 16384-point rectangular FFT of steady state (samples 2N..3N−1); five equal tones on bins 7·{37,101,233,419,677} (0.76–13.9 kHz); memoryless `tanh(D·x)` per oversampled sample. Cases: nominal A=0.1, D=4 (gated); hot A=0.3, D=8 (near hard clip; recorded, not gated). All non-aliased products land on bins ≡0 (mod 7); an alias folded by j·F·Fs or j·Fs lands on a bin ≡ j·F·N (mod 7) ≠ 0 for j∤7 since gcd(7,N)=1. Unwanted energy = in-band (20 Hz–20 kHz) power on bins ≢0 (mod 7), relative to in-band power of the 64x FIR reference (140 dB stages). Excluded: the mod-7 lattice; inseparable: folds with j≥7. Filters: FIR Kaiser, A=target+10 dB, c odd; 4x lengths 147/27 taps, 16x 147/27/23/19. IIR stages 8/4/3/2 coefficients (2x..16x), nonlinear phase, latency reported as DC group delay only.
Finding (T21, open): with the hot stimulus 4x leaves −53 dB of aliasing for both FIR and IIR, so the 4x rate, not the filter, is the limit for a memoryless near-hard clipper. Whether the integrated electrical model meets −70 dB in Studio is unknown until the circuit runs inside the oversampler (G03); remedies (e.g. antiderivative antialiasing or a higher Studio rate) are design decisions not taken here.

Note: spec says soft clip is "bounded below absolute 1"; in double precision tanh saturates to exactly 1.0 for |x|≳2.8. Mathematically strict, numerically ≤1. Reversible interpretation recorded; no threshold weakened.
